### 1. A project with a 3 year life and a cost of €28.000 generates revenues of €8.000 in year 1, €12.000 in year 2, and €17.000 in year 3. If the discount rate is 3%, what is the NPV of the project?

- [ ] €34.635
- [ ] €6.635
- [ ] €6.653
- [ ] €5.860
- [ ] €6.356

> [!success]- Answer
> **€6.635**
>
> NPV = -28000 + 8000/(1,03)^1 + 12000/(1,03)^2 + 17000/(1,03)^3 = -28000 + 7767 + 11311 + 15557 = 6635

### 2. A start-up company is trying to get their first product to market. They set their goals for the project back when they started the company, and haven't revisited them since despite being a less-mature company. The goals were largely focused on "why", "how" and "what" while being specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, and time-bound. How should the start-up improve their goal setting strategy?

- [ ] The start-up should scope the project more thoroughly through the golden circle, as a strong vision will enable the less-mature teams to develop and grow while strengtening the collaboration.
- [ ] The current strategy could be improved by implimenting SMART goals, as these will help specify how the project can contribute to the purpose.
- [ ] They should change their goals to being frequently discussed, ambitious, specific, and transparent, as their current strategy undervalue ambition, focus narrowly on individual performance, and ignore the importance of discussing goals throughout the year.
- [ ] The start-up's goal setting strategy already follows "best practice" and therefore cannot be improved.

> [!success]- Answer
> **They should change their goals to being frequently discussed, ambitious, specific, and transparent, as their current strategy undervalue ambition, focus narrowly on individual performance, and ignore the importance of discussing goals throughout the year.**
>
> The company already has SMART goals, but FAST goals would fit their project better as it is important to discuss the goals frequently and promote ambition — especially in a less mature company. During scoping, they have also already used the golden circle (Why, how, what).

### 6. Anna's project has just finished and they now have a new IT system that automatically handles user requests. This has made the customer support process much more efficient and overall sales have increased by 10%. What is the outcome of the project?

- [ ] A new IT system
- [ ] Overall sales have increased by 10%

> [!success]- Answer
> **Customer support is more efficient**
>
> An outcome is the result of the change derived from using the project's output(s) — not the physical product, and not the measurable benefit of the outcome.

### 10. For a new project with a 3 year lifecycle, you have planned the following cost and revenue structure. Start-up costs 80000; Running costs 35000 / 50000 / 55000; Revenue 30000 / 55000 / 60000; Sale of business 90000. The discount rate is 8%. True or False: from a purely financial perspective this project is worth pursuing.

- [ ] True
- [ ] False

> [!success]- Answer
> **False**
>
> NPV = -80,000 + (30,000-35,000)/(1.08)^1 + (55,000-50,000)/(1.08)^2 + (90,000+60,000-55,000)/(1.08)^3 = -4,929 EUR. The net present value is negative, therefore the project is not worth pursuing financially.

### 11. Mario leads a competent group of electrical engineers. He is a line manager, leading a functional department in a matrix organisation. According to the lecture, when his engineers join a project team, his role is usually to define:

- [ ] What and who
- [ ] What and when
- [ ] Who and how
- [ ] When and how

> [!success]- Answer
> **Who and how**
>
> The line manager defines who will do the job (he knows his people's competencies and availability) and how (the technical competence). The project manager defines what needs to be done, and when.

### 12. Peter is a successful project manager looking for new job opportunities. Which one of the following organisational structures would Peter prefer to work for?

- [ ] Strong matrix organisation or heavyweight project organisation
- [ ] Functional organisation
- [ ] Weak Matrix organisation or lightweight project organisation
- [ ] Matrix organisation

> [!success]- Answer
> **Strong matrix organisation or heavyweight project organisation**
>
> Peter would have more authority, resource availability and control over budget. Projects requiring strong technical competence tend to be better managed in a strong matrix or project-led organisation, so technical people still receive support from their peers.

### 13. Several types of project life-cycles exist. Which of the following statements are correct?

- [ ] Adaptive (agile) project lifecycle is best fitted for projects with well known outcomes.
- [ ] Sequential project lifecycle is best fitted for rapidly changing environments and not well known outcomes.
- [ ] Concurrent project lifecycle is best fitted for large and complex projects with a requirement for fast completion.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Concurrent project lifecycle is best fitted for large and complex projects with a requirement for fast completion.**
>
> Sequential suits well-known outcomes; adaptive (agile) suits rapidly changing environments and not well known outcomes. The other two options swap these round.

### 14. The statements below refer to the magic triangle, also termed the iron triangle. Based on the lecture and the course literature, which one of the statements below is INCORRECT?

- [ ] The iron triangle is often used in practice.
- [ ] The iron triangle is potentially incomplete. Other criteria might also be essential.
- [ ] Projects are about the creation of value, and therefore the iron triangle is irrelevant to any kind of project at any time in its lifecycle.
- [ ] The iron triangle can be based on unrealistic estimations.

> [!success]- Answer
> **Projects are about the creation of value, and therefore the iron triangle is irrelevant to any kind of project at any time in its lifecycle.**
>
> The iron triangle is not irrelevant. It can be very useful in negotiation — for example showing how a scope change will impact budget and time. Despite its limitations it is still widely used in practice.
