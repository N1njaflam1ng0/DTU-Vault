### Introduction: What is uncertainty?

#### Types of uncertainty (Johari window)

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=5&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.5]]

Johari Window: Knowns/unknowns as noun (what we have) and adjective (whether we are aware of it):
- Known-knowns: things we know we know. We can plan with these.
- Known-unknowns: things we know we don't know. Handled by risk management.
- Unknown-unknowns (unk-unks): things we don't know we don't know. Lack of knowledge leads to unexpected events, which leads to change & crises. Handled by issue management / change control when they materialize.
- Unknown-knowns: things we should have known, or don't know we know wrongly. Sources: errors (bad planning, poor communication), biases & emotions, politics.

Objective uncertainty: I know the probability, fx weather conditions
Subjective uncertainty: I guess the probability, fx war in a certain region

#### Uncertainty is what we would like to know (but don't)
The gap between what we know and what we would like to know for a "perfect" decision. The gap consists of:

#### Sources of uncertainty
A spectrum from present/past to future:
Present/Past:
- Missing/wrong facts
- Ambiguity
- Complexity
Future:
- Complexity
- Variability
- Unpredictability(black swan)

#### Uncertainty through the life cycle

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=9&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.9]]

At inception uncertainty is at its highest and information at its lowest; over time information grows and uncertainty falls - but it never reaches zero before completion. Same logic as the paradox of project planning, where base big early decisions based on limited information.

#### Two representations of uncertainty
Aleatory uncertainty (expressed as numbers):
- Can be expressed as probability x impact
- Can be reduced through ARTA (Avoid, Reduce, Transfer, Accept) - risk management

Epistemic uncertainty (lack of knowledge):
- Hard to put into numbers
- About the future and present (and past)
- Can be reduced through gaining more knowledge

#### Uncertainty perspective (summary)
- All projects face uncertainty in their decision-making
- Uncertainty is the absence of decision-relevant knowledge regarding past, present and future
- Uncertainty is reduced over time but never fully eliminated
- It can relate to things we could know, could estimate, or unpredictable developments & events
- Uncertainty is unavoidable in projects and requires smart & active management

### Anticipating: What if something happens?

#### What is a risk?
- "Effect of uncertainty on objectives" (ISO 31000). An uncertain event or condition that, if it occurs, has a positive or negative effect on one or more project objectives such as time, cost and quality.
- ISO 21502: risks are uncertain events that may have positive (opportunities) or negative (threats) effects on project objectives.
	- Opportunity: risk occurrence that would have a favourable impact
	- Threat: risk occurrence that would have a negative impact

- Failure of project managers to assess and manage risks is a major cause of project failure.
- Risk traditionally described as: Risk = probability of event x impact of event. Comes from finance/insurance, where risk relates to one objective (money) backed by statistical data. Fx car insurance: pooling many clients makes the expected payout reliable - but project risks are usually one-offs without statistics.

How risk relates to uncertainty: 
- Under certainty a decision leads to the desired outcome. 
- Under uncertainty a decision leads to the desired OR an undesired outcome - the undesired branch is the risk.

Etymology: 
- Greek rhizikon ("difficulty to avoid at sea")
- Arabic rizk, and middle-high-German Rysigo ("to dare, to undertake, hope for economic success"). 
- The Chinese word for risk combines the symbols for "danger" and "opportunity" - risk is both threat and opportunity.

#### Purpose of risk management
- Inform decisions and focus management attention
- Encourage proactive rather than reactive management
- Risk management is an ongoing process (risk management = project management!)
- Systematic approach - not only to avoid risk, but to recognize it, price it and sell it
- Find an appropriate balance between risk and return

#### Risk according to ISO 21502
The purpose of risk management is to increase the likelihood of achieving the project's objectives.

Activities:
- 7.8.2 Identifying risk
- 7.8.3 Assessing risk
- 7.8.4 Treating risk
- 7.8.5 Controlling risk

#### Classic risk management process
Identification, Assessment/Analysis, Response, Documentation/Monitor. 

The slides use the strip: Identify, Assess, Treat, Control. Risk assessment takes place several times throughout the life cycle - it does not stop after initiation.

#### Identify: tools
- Documentation review
- Information gathering (brainstorming)
- Risk catalogue / risk categories (generic checklists of risk areas: market, strategy, finance, processes/organization/personnel...)
- Assumption analysis
- Risk breakdown structure
- Pre-mortem analysis
- Diagramming techniques, fx fishbone diagram (structured cause identification, grouped by themes)
- Risk workshop - brainstorming
- SWOT analysis (strengths/weaknesses are internal, opportunities/threats are external)

#### Workshops (risk brainstorming)
DOs:
- Be creative and allow out-of-the-box thinking
- Collect/document/visualize ideas
- Make sure everyone is heard
- Share and build on each other's ideas (IDEO rules: defer judgement, encourage wild ideas, build with "and" instead of "but", one conversation at a time).

DON'Ts: 
- Rely only on a risk checklist
- Judge or criticize during brainstorming (review & sort after brainstorm).
- Stick with one or a few risk areas.
- Let a few people do all the talking.

The difficulty is NOT to identify a large number of risks - risk management is also about focusing attention.

#### Formulating risks + risk register
A risk register documents the risks:
- Be specific: instead of "poor communication" (a root cause), write the effect, fx "client's requirements are misunderstood"
- Use a naming nomenclature so risks can be compared
- Document status and risk responsible (the simple example below is missing these!)

Formula: If (event) occurs due to (driver), the consequences could result in (impact).

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=37&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.37]]

#### Assess: probability and impact
Purpose of risk management: measure and prioritize the risks for further action, and understand the overall risk situation and its development.

Risk management processes:
- Estimate the probability/likelihood of each risk and the corresponding impact on project objectives if it occurs
- Prioritize according to fx timeframe and key stakeholders' risk tolerance
- Probability either as a statistical distribution or as qualitative categories: impossible, low, medium, high, certain
- Impact can hit ANY project objective (not just time/cost/quality)
- Risk map: plot risks on probability vs impact to communicate priorities

Be wary! Assessment is often based on expert judgement (for lack of historical data or reliable facts) with deliberate and/or unconscious biases. A solid qualification is better than a shaky quantification!

#### Treat: responses to threats and opportunities

| Threat response | Generic strategy | Opportunity response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Avoid | Eliminate uncertainty | Exploit |
| Transfer | Distribute uncertainty | Share |
| Reduce | Change likelihood | Enhance |
| Accept | Include in baseline | Ignore |
Double probability-impact matrix - threats and opportunities mirrored in one map:
![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=51&rect=17,72,929,500|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.51]]

"Risk treatment should be appropriate to the risk, cost-effective, timely, realistic within the project context, understood by all parties involved and assigned to an appropriate person." (ISO 21500)

ARTA for threats:
- Avoid: eliminate the uncertainty - make the risk impossible (probability to zero) or execute the project differently so it is insulated from the effect (impact to zero)
- Reduce: reduce probability and/or impact to make the risk acceptable
- Transfer: pass liability and responsibility to another stakeholder better able to manage the risk (fx insurance, contracts)
- Accept: recognize that residual risks must be taken - actively (allocate contingency) or passively (just monitor)

The response takes the gross risk down to the residual risk.

For opportunities (mirror image):
- Exploit: make the opportunity definitely happen (probability to 100%)
- Enhance: increase probability and/or impact to maximize the benefit
- Share: find a partner able to manage the opportunity, sharing the upside
- Ignore: no active measures, reactive approach for minor opportunities

Remember: the process helps you focus effort on the MOST RELEVANT risks and opportunities, not to avoid/exploit everything. There is an optimal intensity of risk mitigation - total cost = cost of risk + cost of mitigation:

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=52&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.52]]

#### Contingency plan
Sometimes the only sensible response is accept + be prepared for when it happens:
- For catastrophic but very unlikely events (terror attack, major earthquake, bank default)
- When you know the rough consequence but not the specific threat, fx a death on a construction site causing catastrophic reputational damage - have a communication strategy ready in the drawer

#### Tools & techniques of control
- Quality: conformance / performance
- Cost and time
- Earned Value
- Risks
- Visual control
- War rooms

Quality conformance: a formalised system ensuring the needs of the customer are continually met - policies, system description, procedures. Protects the organisation, prerequisite for obtaining business. Quality standards fx ISO 9000.

Quality performance: control of stakeholder expectations and perceptions is vital for customer satisfaction. Don't create expectations that cannot be delivered ("it does exactly what it says on the tin"); measure expectations and perceptions, small timely changes bring perceptions back in line.

Cost and time are interlinked. Progress is often considered in terms of sunk costs (what has been spent so far) - can be unreliable; Earned Value is more meaningful.

#### Earned Value Analysis (EVA) - EXAM
You cannot assess project status by looking at money spent OR schedule adherence alone:
- Only accrued costs: a delay may look like underspending (less work was done)
- Only work done vs schedule: no information on the cost side

EVA combines both: are we on track to over- or underspend, considering the progress of the work? Requires a detailed plan (planned cost curve over time) + tracking of actual costs + measuring actual progress.

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=71&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.71]]

The three curves:
- PV = Planned Value = BCWS (Budgeted Cost of Work Scheduled): what should have been spent by now according to plan
- EV = Earned Value = BCWP (Budgeted Cost of Work Performed): what the completed work was budgeted to cost
- AC = Actual Cost = ACWP (Actual Cost of Work Performed): what has actually been spent
- BAC = Budget at Completion

Status formulas (EV always first!):

| Name | Formula | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost Variance (CV) | EV - AC | Negative = over budget, positive = under budget |
| Schedule Variance (SV) | EV - PV | Negative = behind schedule, positive = ahead |
| Cost Performance Index (CPI) | EV / AC | How much are we getting for every dollar we spend? |
| Schedule Performance Index (SPI) | EV / PV | Progress as % against plan |
| Estimate to Complete (ETC) | EAC - AC | How much more do we have to spend? |
| Variance at Completion (VAC) | BAC - EAC | At the end of the day, how close will we be to plan? |

NB: the diagram on p.71 defines the variances the other way around (CV = ACWP - BCWP, SV = BCWS - BCWP, plus AV = accounting variance = BCWS - ACWP). Use the standard convention above (EV minus something), where negative = bad.

Status can be plotted as CPI vs SPI quadrants (ahead/behind schedule x under-/overspent):

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=72&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.72]]

EV process: 
- set the budget per activity (time, materials, overheads)
- measure progress per activity (actual vs planned spend)
- compare
- calculate variances/indices/estimates to completion.

Percent complete is difficult to measure - rules of thumb per activity:
- 50/50 rule: 50% credit when task started, final 50% at completion
- 20/80 rule: 20% at start, 80% at completion
- 0/100 rule: no credit until complete

EXTRA - forecasting the Estimate at Completion (EAC):

| Formula | Use when |
| --- | --- |
| BAC / CPI | No variances from BAC have occurred / variances are typical |
| AC + ATC | Original estimate was bad (actuals + new estimate) |
| AC + BAC - EV | Current variances are not expected to continue |
| AC + (BAC - EV) / CPI | Current variances are expected to continue |

Tricks for remembering EV:
- EV is always first and is earned value
- Variance = EV minus something
- Index = EV divided by something
- If the formula relates to cost use AC; if it relates to schedule use PV
- Interpreting: negative is bad, positive is good; greater than 1 is good, less than 1 is bad

#### Monitor risks
Risk management does not stop after initiation!
- Update the risk register: track identified risks, identify and analyze new risks - periodically, when a new risk arises, or when a milestone is reached
- Review risk treatment: progress on treatments and their effectiveness

#### Visual control
If you can see it, you can monitor it: visible signs (lights indicating problems, output vs target), 

Gantt chart highlighting critical path progress, traffic lights, digital dashboards, war rooms all rely on timeliness and veracity of data.

#### Control systems
Designing control systems - as simple as possible but not simpler:
- Low complexity projects: basic control measures
- Medium/high complexity: the PM's role in establishing and maintaining the control system is paramount
- Total control is widely promoted but not simple and may not be desirable

Think carefully: 
- WHAT needs to be controlled (which variables, acceptable deviation).
- HOW to control it (can we measure and trust the data, how to get back on track).
- Maximise visibility of progress.
- Behavioural aspect: Different people demand different degrees of control, and controlcontrol changes working conditions (project driven by fear) and focuses people's attention

#### Project status reporting (EXTRA)
Teams report status to decision-makers (steering committee, client, risk owners) to ensure risk transparency, facilitate decision-making and increase risk culture. 
Four principles:
- Materiality: report only significant risks and KPIs
- Timeliness: allow responses in due time
- Consistency: same format & KPIs every time for comparability
- Accuracy: appropriate level of accuracy even though based on estimates

### Adapting: Can we keep our project relevant?

#### Deviation and corrective action
Managing projects is like driving a car: detect and correct deviations from the planned course as early as possible - but avoid over-reaction. A single event is the product of many linked smaller events, so monitor and act on the smaller events too. Feedforward = look for problems that may be faced and change to protect the outcomes (= risk management).

#### Issues and change control according to ISO 21502
Issue management (7.9): resolve issues such that there is no negative impact on the achievement of the project's objectives.
- 7.9.2 Identifying issues
- 7.9.3 Resolving issues

Change control (7.10): control changes to the project and deliverables, and formalize acceptance or rejection of these changes.
- 7.10.2 Establishing a change control framework
- 7.10.3 Identifying and assessing change requests
- 7.10.4 Planning the implementation of change requests
- 7.10.5 Implementing and closing change requests

#### Strategies to reduce the impact of high uncertainty
- Create flexibility in plans or contracts (fx cost-plus contracts, Last Planner, agile)
- Keep your options open: change direction/objectives based on results of earlier phases, adapt to external changes (change request management, contract management)
- Remain able to kill bad projects or re-prioritize them (fx stage gate model)
- OR: push the impact of uncertainty to the other party (power-play, fx fixed price contracts)

#### Last Planner (lean construction)
- AGAINST the idea that detailed planning by a central office remote from the work is more accurate - proposes the opposite: those who perform the task do the planning
- Look-ahead schedules 4-6 weeks in advance; weekly schedules with activities in half-day units
- Allows exploration of dependencies (frequently overlooked); review the following week
- Measure with Planned Percent Complete: PPC = activities completed / intended completed activities x 100
- Discuss causes if not 100%; PPC expected to improve as estimating gets better

#### Agile
Management method designed for high uncertainty or very dynamic environments. Key principles:
- Flexible strategy
- Interdisciplinary teams
- Short cycles (sprints)
- Visualization

#### Managing uncertainty through contracting
Same relationship spectrum as in Complexity (spot buy to alliance, see Complexity notes p.107):
- Cost-plus / partnership end: creates flexibility to cope with a lot of uncertainty
- Fixed price end: pushes cost uncertainty entirely to the supplier (non-delivery/bad quality often included as supplier penalties)

#### Scope creep and change requests
1. Get agreement on scope before you launch
2. Choose a technique to manage scope creep:
	- Low uncertainty: make a list of what is in scope (and what is not)
	- High uncertainty: set scope tolerance parameters and learn as the project progresses
3. Get stakeholders to sign off or review the parameters

Change requests (contract management): the PM reviews changes from the client for their effect on overall objectives (sometimes you can cope by shuffling things around). If a change significantly affects key contractual objectives, initiate the change request process defined in the contract. Many projects have a contract manager tracking requested changes and their effect on scope, cost and schedule. Fx: client requires an extra feature (more cost/time), client wants delivery a month earlier (reduced scope or added resources), internal re-prioritization reduces resource availability (longer schedule).

### Learning: How can we know?

#### The paradox of project planning
Important decisions are made at the start, when available knowledge and information is lowest. Stage gates and learning are ways to handle it.

#### Stage gates
Phased approach to manage high uncertainty and avoid getting locked in. Simplest form: a feasibility study as a mini-project before committing (creates knowledge, reduces uncertainty). The formal stage gate process (from new product development): the project is reviewed at gates after each stage, and the gate keepers (fx steering committee) decide Go / Stop (kill) / Hold / Recycle.

At each gate there is a review and a go, stop or kill is decided.
![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=109&rect=23,40,936,177|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.109]]


#### The learning plan
If you can't plan(high uncertainty), you must learn. A competency of experimentation: the ability to experiment with technology and business concepts/models simultaneously to arrive at a demonstrated model of a new business that brings breakthrough value.
Can target known unknowns.

#### Lessons learned
At the end of the project, a close-down meeting reflects on what went well, what went badly, what was unexpected - lessons that improve future projects.
- Set up structures to capture lessons timely and structured (what happened and why? why different from expected?)
- Document so outsiders can understand them later
- Make lessons findable (common database, keywords)
- Formulate so the learning can be applied (what has to change, how, why and when?)
- Particularly interesting projects can become internal training case studies

Lessons learned according to ISO 21502 (7.18): benefit from experience, avoid repeating mistakes and disseminate improved practices.
- 7.18.2 Identifying lessons
- 7.18.3 Disseminating lessons

### Deciding: What should we do?

#### Projects need constant decision-making
Projects are novel & unique (in some regard), with many unspecified areas & procedures - Where? What? How? Who? When? Goal-directed vehicles for change. Fx the hiring exercise (Per vs Kelly): even with good information the decision is hard, because criteria conflict and information is incomplete.

#### Decision making under risk: expected value
If uncertainty can be quantified as risk (ideally financial), the "rational" approach is a cost-benefit analysis with expected values. Fx a bet: 10% chance of winning 200 DKK, 90% chance of losing 20 DKK:
- EV of benefits: 0.1 x 200 = 20 DKK
- EV of losses: 0.9 x (-20) = -18 DKK
- EV of the bet = 2 DKK, so positive - take the bet (over infinite repetitions)

Four limitations in project reality:
1. Our minds are biased about gains vs losses (see Interpreting)
2. Most project risks are one-offs - no infinite repetitions
3. We often know neither probability nor impact
4. Different objectives can't all be broken down into the same unit (fx money)

#### Decision-making under uncertainty
When you do not have values for probability and impact:
- Scenario analysis: 
	- compare potential outcomes of options (worst case / most likely / best case)
	- choose according to risk tolerance (minimize regret, minimize worst case, maximize best case)
- Satisficing: 
	- choose the first option that fits minimum requirements 
	- If "good enough" is good enough, a quick solution is needed
	- you understand your minimum requirements
- Decision matrix: 
	- compare options on their most likely outcome
	- suitable for very different decision factors
	- when you understand what is important and how options perform

#### Decision matrix
Options as rows, factors as columns. Score each option per factor (0-5), weight each factor by importance (0-5), sum score x weight per option - highest total wins.

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=126&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.126]]

HOWEVER: be careful how scores and weights are derived - a decision matrix is very easy to manipulate, as the values may be very subjective.

EXTRA - Choosing by Advantages (CBA): decision system that avoids double-counting by deciding based on the importance of advantages only (not advantages and disadvantages).

### Interpreting: What is our/their perspective?

#### Error, lie, or misunderstanding?
In hindsight many project decisions seem "bad" - but could the decision makers have decided better with what they COULD know at the time? Three explanations:
- Error: cognitive biases/limitations, human error, groupthink
- Lie: opportunistic behaviour, strategic misrepresentation
- Misunderstanding: differences in preferences, values, beliefs, interpretations

#### Bias and errors
We do not act rationally by default - it takes effort and structured methods.
- System 1: easy and automatic, judges by familiar patterns
- System 2: difficult and time-consuming, deliberate and methodic
- We need to activate system 2 (structured processes, due diligence, discipline) and recognize the emotional side of risks. No silver bullets - work on risk attitude and risk culture.

Most common cognitive biases in projects: 
- self-efficacy bias/overconfidence, 
- illusion of control
- sunk cost bias
- recency bias
- anchoring

All leading to escalation of commitment and over-optimism.

#### Risk perception - risk as a feeling

| Question | Increases risk perception | Decreases risk perception |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How (un)desirable is the impact? | Dread risks (high losses) | Desired outcome (high benefits, over-optimism) |
| When will the impact materialize? (prospect theory) | Soon | Far in the future |
| How familiar is the risk & context? | Unfamiliar/novel | Well-known |
| When did it happen last? (recency/hot hand bias) | Just in the last project | It's been a while... |
| Do you have control over the risk? (illusion of control) | No control | (Subjectively perceived) control |

The recency effect also works for opportunities; the opposite effect exists too (gambler's fallacy - after a streak of bad luck you expect your luck to change).

Personality matters: a risk-seeking person accepts risks that a risk-averse person would avoid - the accept/avoid boundary sits differently on the probability-impact matrix. Also remember the optimal effort curve (p.52): both too little AND too much risk mitigation is costly.

#### Strategic use of uncertainty
Flyvbjerg: uncertainty can be used strategically - understated costs + overstated benefits get projects approved (strategic misrepresentation, see also Estimations are political in Complexity and the two schools table in Introduction).

### Connecting the dots

#### Managing uncertainty through planning
"In preparing for battle, I have always found that plans are useless, but planning is indispensable." (Eisenhower)

#### The uncertainty perspective: three roles
- Identify & reduce uncertainty through planning and knowledge gathering
- Develop management strategies to cope with identified uncertainty
- Manage unexpected developments and materializing risks

Key concerns:
- Be proactive: uncertainty is no excuse for reactive management - proactive management is the purpose of risk and opportunity management
- Control, adapt and learn: we cannot and should not prevent all risks - monitor progress and adapt to changes
- Tolerate: all projects are inherently uncertain; tolerate a level of uncertainty and manage its gradual reduction through the life cycle

#### What should we do about uncertainty?

![[Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023.pdf#page=149&rect=-2,36,949,535|Uncertainty-slides-Jan%202023, p.149]]

Three stances - a matter of philosophy (and context):
- Reduce (Plan & Control)
- Embrace (Monitor & Adapt)
- Accept (Prepare & Respond)

Guiding questions: can we identify all relevant risks? Is it worth the effort? Can we prevent them anyway? Is risk anticipation an efficient use of resources? Is there value in quantifying risks?

#### Resilient projects
Rigid objectives and plans make projects brittle - anything off-plan quickly becomes a crisis. Strategies to improve resilience:
- Several independent objectives that can make the project a success (fx Norwegian tunnel that also served as a quarry - failed as tunnel, succeeded as quarry)
- Several independent approaches to reach the objective (fx Manhattan project: parallel R&D projects with the same objective)
- Plan so objectives can be adapted throughout (agile; fx Tagus River Bridge designed to be retrofitted as traffic grew)
- Create learning opportunities before the critical project (fx Heathrow T5 roof intentionally built twice - once off site)
- Start only when circumstances are favourable (low prices, expressed client interest)
- Break the project into incremental steps where you can stop at any time (fx modular software with a series of releases)

"Life can only be understood backwards; but it must be lived forwards." (Kierkegaard)

#### Points for reflection → use for the case analysis (Tove Skou Roer report)
- Anticipating: what were the main risks in the project? How did they manage the threats and opportunities?
- Monitoring: what tools did the project manager use to get information about progress? Who was involved in the controlling process?
- Adapting: how did changing environments influence the project? How was the PM adapting to change, and by which means did the project adapt?
- Learning: how did they build upon experience and knowledge from former projects? How was the project organized to facilitate learning? How was knowledge transferred to other projects?
- Deciding: what strategies were used for making decisions? What were the consequences? Could they have improved the knowledge base before deciding?
- Interpreting: how were decisions biased in the project? What were the attitudes towards risks? Did the project initiators use strategic misrepresentation to get approval?
