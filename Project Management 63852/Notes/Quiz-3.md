# PART 1 — ISO 21500:2021, Clause 3: Terms and Definitions

**Q1.** According to ISO 21500, a _benefit_ is defined as:

- A) A financial return exceeding the investment
- B) Created advantage, value or other positive effect
- C) The realised outcome of a project's outputs
- D) A measurable improvement in stakeholder satisfaction

**Answer: B** — "Created advantage, value or other positive effect." (Sourced from ISO/TR 21506)

**Q2.** A _business case_ is defined as:

- A) A financial model justifying investment
- B) Documented justification to support decision making about the commitment to a project, programme or portfolio
- C) The strategic rationale for a portfolio
- D) A written contract between sponsor and project manager

**Answer: B**

**Q3.** A _deliverable_ is:

- A) Any tangible product handed to the customer
- B) An aggregated set of outputs
- C) A unique and verifiable element that is required to be produced by a project
- D) The change resulting from a project's output

**Answer: C**

**Q4.** _Governance_ is defined as:

- A) The management of projects, programmes and portfolios
- B) Principles, policies and framework by which an organization is directed and controlled
- C) Oversight exercised by the project board
- D) Rules for authorising expenditure

**Answer: B** — Note the key words: _directed and controlled_.

**Q5.** A _governing body_ is:

- A) The project board
- B) The sponsoring organization
- C) A person, group or entity accountable for the governance of an organization, organizations or a part of an organization
- D) The senior management team of a company

**Answer: C**

**Q6.** In ISO 21500, an _opportunity_ is:

- A) A chance to gain competitive advantage
- B) An unplanned positive outcome
- C) A risk occurrence that would have a favourable impact
- D) A benefit not identified in the business case

**Answer: C**

**Q7.** A _threat_ is:

- A) An issue requiring escalation
- B) A risk occurrence that would have a negative impact
- C) An external factor outside the organization's control
- D) A constraint on the project

**Answer: B**

**Q8.** An _outcome_ is defined as:

- A) The result of the project at closure
- B) Change resulting from the use of the output from a project
- C) Aggregated deliverables
- D) The benefits realised by stakeholders

**Answer: B** — The key word is **change**, and it results from _use_ of the output.

**Q9.** An _output_ is defined as:

- A) Any product created during the project
- B) The change resulting from the project
- C) Aggregated tangible or intangible deliverables that form the project result
- D) The measurable value delivered to the sponsor

**Answer: C**

**Q10.** Put the chain in the correct order as used by ISO 21500/21502:

- A) Outcomes → deliverables → outputs → benefits
- B) Deliverables → outputs → outcomes → benefits
- C) Benefits → outputs → deliverables → outcomes
- D) Outputs → deliverables → benefits → outcomes

**Answer: B** — Deliverables aggregate into outputs; using outputs creates outcomes (change); outcomes realise benefits.

**Q11.** A _portfolio_ is:

- A) A group of related projects managed in a coordinated way
- B) A collection of portfolio components grouped together to facilitate their management to meet strategic objectives
- C) All the projects an organization is running
- D) A set of investments prioritised by return

**Answer: B**

**Q12.** A _portfolio component_ can be:

- A) Only projects and programmes
- B) Only projects
- C) A project, programme, portfolio or other related work
- D) A work package or deliverable

**Answer: C** — Note: a portfolio can contain another portfolio.

**Q13.** A _programme component_ can be:

- A) A project, programme or other related work
- B) A project, programme, portfolio or other related work
- C) Only projects
- D) Projects and work packages

**Answer: A** — Careful: programme components exclude _portfolios_; portfolio components include them.

**Q14.** _Portfolio management_ is defined as:

- A) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of strategic objectives
- B) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of agreed objectives
- C) Coordinated activities to direct and control the realisation of identified benefits and deliverables
- D) Selecting and prioritising projects

**Answer: A** — **Strategic** objectives = portfolio.

**Q15.** _Programme management_ is defined as:

- A) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of strategic objectives
- B) Coordinated activities to direct and control the realisation of identified benefits and deliverables
- C) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of agreed objectives
- D) Managing a group of projects towards a shared outcome

**Answer: B** — **Benefits and deliverables** = programme.

**Q16.** _Project management_ is defined as:

- A) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of agreed objectives
- B) Coordinated activities to direct and control the accomplishment of strategic objectives
- C) The application of tools and techniques to meet requirements
- D) Coordinated activities to realise benefits

**Answer: A** — **Agreed** objectives = project.

**Q17.** A _programme_ is:

- A) A collection of components grouped to meet strategic objectives
- B) A group of programme components managed in a coordinated way to realize benefits
- C) A long-running project
- D) A set of projects with a shared sponsor

**Answer: B**

**Q18.** A _project_ is defined as:

- A) A temporary endeavour to achieve one or more defined objectives
- B) A temporary endeavour created to produce agreed deliverables
- C) A unique undertaking with a start and end date
- D) A temporary organization delivering value

**Answer: A** — Note: option B is the _old_ ISO/TR 21506 wording, which was modified.

**Q19.** A _sponsor_ is:

- A) The person who funds the project
- B) The person responsible for obtaining the resources and executive decisions to enable success
- C) The chair of the project board
- D) The owner of the business case

**Answer: B**

**Q20.** A _stakeholder_ is:

- A) Anyone affected by the project
- B) Person, group or organization that has interests in, or can affect, be affected by, or perceive itself to be affected by, any aspect of the project, programme or portfolio
- C) Any person with a financial stake in the outcome
- D) Any internal or external party named in the business case

**Answer: B** — Note the crucial phrase **"or perceive itself to be affected by."**

**Q21.** How many terms are defined in ISO 21500:2021 Clause 3?

- A) 15
- B) 19
- C) 30
- D) 42

**Answer: B** — 3.1 to 3.19.

**Q22.** Which of these terms is defined in ISO 21500 Clause 3?

- A) Work breakdown structure
- B) Critical path
- C) Governing body
- D) Baseline

**Answer: C** — A, B and D appear in ISO 21502 Clause 3, not in ISO 21500 Clause 3.

**Q23.** Which source does ISO 21500 cite for the definition of _project_?

- A) ISO/TR 21506:2018
- B) ISO 21502:2020
- C) ISO 9000:2015
- D) ISO 21505

**Answer: B** — In ISO 21500, _project_, _project management_, _deliverable_, _outcome_ and _output_ are sourced from ISO 21502:2020; most other terms come from ISO/TR 21506:2018.

**Q24.** Which document is the vocabulary standard for project, programme and portfolio management?

- A) ISO 21500
- B) ISO 21505
- C) ISO/TR 21506
- D) ISO 21511

**Answer: C**

**Q25.** In ISO 21500's definitions, which term is described as "risk occurrence" — twice, with opposite impacts?

- A) Issue and problem
- B) Opportunity and threat
- C) Benefit and dis-benefit
- D) Outcome and output

**Answer: B**

**Q26.** Which statement about _sponsor_ vs _governing body_ is correct per ISO 21500?

- A) The sponsor is always a member of the governing body
- B) The governing body is accountable for governance; the sponsor obtains resources and executive decisions to enable success
- C) They are synonymous
- D) The governing body reports to the sponsor

**Answer: B**

**Q27.** Which of the following is NOT part of the ISO 21500 definition of stakeholder?

- A) Has interests in the project
- B) Can affect the project
- C) Perceives itself to be affected
- D) Has legal ownership of a share in the organization

**Answer: D** — That describes a _shareholder_ (slides distinguish these).

**Q28.** In ISO 21500, _business case_ supports decision making about commitment to:

- A) A project only
- B) A project or programme
- C) A project, programme or portfolio
- D) A portfolio only

**Answer: C**

**Q29.** ISO 21500 defines a portfolio as grouped "to facilitate their management to meet…":

- A) Agreed objectives
- B) Strategic objectives
- C) Benefit realisation
- D) Stakeholder expectations

**Answer: B**

**Q30.** True or false: Under ISO 21500, "other related work" can be a portfolio component.

- A) True
- B) False

**Answer: A (True)**

# PART 2 — ISO 21502:2020, Clause 3: Terms and Definitions

**Q31.** A _baseline_ is:

- A) The approved plan at project start
- B) Reference basis for comparison against which performance is monitored and controlled
- C) The original budget and schedule
- D) The agreed scope statement

**Answer: B**

**Q32.** A _change request_ is:

- A) A formal instruction to alter the baseline
- B) Documentation that defines a proposed alteration to a project
- C) A request to modify the business case
- D) A record of an approved change

**Answer: B** — Note: it is a _proposed_ alteration, i.e. before authorisation.

**Q33.** _Configuration management_ is defined as:

- A) The management of versions of the project plan
- B) Application of procedures to control, correlate and maintain documentation, specifications and physical attributes
- C) A subset of change control
- D) The control of deliverables during handover

**Answer: B**

**Q34.** _Control_ is defined as:

- A) Monitoring project progress
- B) Comparison of actual performance with planned performance, analysing variances and taking appropriate corrective and preventive action as needed
- C) Authorising or rejecting change requests
- D) The exercise of authority over the project team

**Answer: B**

**Q35.** _Corrective action_ is:

- A) Action to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformity
- B) Direction and activity for modifying the performance of work to bring performance in line with a plan
- C) A response to an identified risk
- D) A change request implemented after a gate review

**Answer: B**

**Q36.** _Preventive action_ is:

- A) Action to eliminate the cause of a potential nonconformity or other potential undesirable situation
- B) Action to bring performance back in line with the plan
- C) Risk mitigation planning
- D) Quality assurance activity

**Answer: A** — Note 1: preventive action prevents **occurrence**; corrective action prevents **recurrence**. (Sourced from ISO 9000:2015)

**Q37.** _Critical path_ is:

- A) The longest sequence of activities in the project
- B) Sequence of activities that determine the earliest possible completion date for a project or phase
- C) The path with zero float
- D) The chain of activities with the highest risk

**Answer: B**

**Q38.** In ISO 21502, an _issue_ is:

- A) A risk that has occurred
- B) Event that arises during a project requiring resolution for the project to proceed
- C) A deviation from the baseline
- D) A stakeholder complaint

**Answer: B**

**Q39.** _Project assurance_ is defined as:

- A) Independent audit of the project accounts
- B) Planned and systematic actions necessary to provide confidence to the sponsoring organization and project sponsor that a project is likely to achieve its objectives
- C) Quality assurance activities applied to deliverables
- D) A governance function of the project board

**Answer: B**

**Q40.** _Project governance_ is defined as:

- A) Principles, policies and framework by which an organization is directed and controlled
- B) Principles, policies and procedures by which a project is authorized and directed to accomplish agreed objectives
- C) The oversight activities of the sponsoring organization
- D) The framework for making decisions on change requests

**Answer: B** — Note the difference from _governance_ (organization-level, "framework", "directed and controlled").

**Q41.** _Project life cycle_ is:

- A) The period from initiation to closure
- B) Defined set of phases from the start to the end of a project
- C) The sequence of gates and decision points
- D) The delivery approach used

**Answer: B**

**Q42.** _Project scope_ is:

- A) The deliverables to be produced
- B) Authorized work to accomplish agreed objectives
- C) The boundaries of the project
- D) The requirements agreed with the customer

**Answer: B** — Modified from ISO/TR 21506: "deliverables" was replaced by "objectives".

**Q43.** A _work breakdown structure_ is:

- A) A hierarchical list of activities and durations
- B) Decomposition of the defined scope of a project or programme into progressively lower levels consisting of elements of work
- C) A chart of the project organisation
- D) A list of work packages assigned to leaders

**Answer: B**

**Q44.** A _work package_ is:

- A) A group of activities that have a defined scope, deliverable, timescale and cost
- B) The lowest level of the WBS
- C) A set of tasks assigned to one person
- D) A phase of the project life cycle

**Answer: A**

**Q45.** How many terms are defined in ISO 21502:2020 Clause 3?

- A) 19
- B) 24
- C) 30
- D) 36

**Answer: C** — 3.1 to 3.30.

**Q46.** Which term appears in ISO 21502 Clause 3 but NOT in ISO 21500 Clause 3?

- A) Sponsor
- B) Stakeholder
- C) Project assurance
- D) Benefit

**Answer: C**

**Q47.** Which of these terms has **no** [SOURCE:] reference in ISO 21502 Clause 3 (i.e. it is defined originally in that standard)?

- A) Baseline
- B) Issue
- C) Portfolio
- D) Sponsor

**Answer: B** — _Issue_, _outcome_, _output_, _project assurance_, _project life cycle_ and _work package_ have no source reference in ISO 21502.

**Q48.** ISO 21502's definition of _deliverable_ was modified from ISO/TR 21506 by replacing:

- A) "objectives" with "deliverables"
- B) "tangible or intangible outcome of a planned activity" with "element that is required to be produced by a project"
- C) "unique" with "verifiable"
- D) "output" with "outcome"

**Answer: B**

**Q49.** ISO 21502's definition of _project_ was modified from ISO/TR 21506 by replacing:

- A) "created to produce agreed deliverables" with "to achieve one or more defined objectives"
- B) "temporary" with "unique"
- C) "endeavour" with "undertaking"
- D) "defined objectives" with "agreed deliverables"

**Answer: A**

**Q50.** Which term is defined as "coordinated activities to direct and **control** the accomplishment of agreed objectives" in ISO 21502?

- A) Project governance
- B) Project management
- C) Project assurance
- D) Control

**Answer: B** — In ISO 21502 the definition explicitly cross-references _control_ (3.6).

# PART 3 — ISO 21502, Clause 4.5: Project Organization and Roles

**Q51.** ISO 21502 describes the project organization as:

- A) A permanent structure within the sponsoring organization
- B) A temporary structure that defines roles, responsibilities and authorities in the project
- C) The set of stakeholders involved in the project
- D) The reporting hierarchy of the project team

**Answer: B**

**Q52.** According to 4.5.1, individuals are assigned to roles in the project organization by:

- A) Function
- B) Name
- C) Competence level
- D) Availability

**Answer: B** — "Individuals are assigned **by names** to specific roles."

**Q53.** The project organization should (select all that apply — which set is complete?):

- A) Specify clear reporting lines; be approved by the project sponsor or project board; be communicated to everyone involved in the project
- B) Specify clear reporting lines; be approved by the governing body; be published externally
- C) Be approved by the project manager; be documented in the business case; be reviewed at each gate
- D) Be defined by HR; be approved by the sponsor; be fixed for the whole project

**Answer: A**

**Q54.** Design of the project organization can depend on:

- A) The project's context, the organizational environment and the stakeholders
- B) The budget, the schedule and the scope
- C) The delivery approach and the life cycle only
- D) The governing body's preferences

**Answer: A**

**Q55.** Per 4.5.1, the design and implementation of project organizations should also consider:

- A) Only formal reporting structures
- B) The informal aspects of project management, such as culture, motivation, co-ordination, interpersonal skills and behaviours
- C) Contractual obligations only
- D) The RACI matrix

**Answer: B**

**Q56.** Which combination of roles must an individual NOT hold simultaneously, according to ISO 21502?

- A) Project manager and work package leader
- B) Project sponsor and project manager (or work package leader / team member)
- C) Project board member and sponsor
- D) Project manager and project team member

**Answer: B** — Because of the possibility of conflicting interests. (Note: the project manager _can_ assume the role of work package leader.)

**Q57.** The project organization can change throughout the project life cycle:

- A) Never — it must remain fixed after approval
- B) Only if a change request is raised
- C) Especially between phases, according to specific works to be accomplished and competencies needed
- D) Only at the request of the governing body

**Answer: C**

**Q58.** The _sponsoring organization_ (4.5.2):

- A) Delivers the project's work packages
- B) Acts as a higher-level authority; provides direction and resources to the project board or sponsor, addresses escalated risk and issues, and makes or refers decisions above the delegated authority of the board/sponsor
- C) Is always the customer organization
- D) Is the same as the governing body of the project

**Answer: B**

**Q59.** For a project within a portfolio, the higher-level authority can be:

- A) The programme manager
- B) The portfolio manager or director
- C) The project board
- D) The governing body

**Answer: B** — For a project within a _programme_, it can be the programme manager.

**Q60.** Which integrated practices are associated with the sponsoring organization?

- A) 6.2 Pre-project activities, 6.3 Overseeing a project, 6.9 Post-project activities
- B) 6.4 Directing a project
- C) 6.5, 6.6 and 6.8
- D) 6.7 Managing delivery

**Answer: A**

**Q61.** Which integrated practice is associated with the project sponsor?

- A) 6.3 Overseeing a project
- B) 6.4 Directing a project
- C) 6.7 Managing delivery
- D) 6.2 Pre-project activities

**Answer: B**

**Q62.** Which integrated practices are associated with the project manager?

- A) 6.2, 6.3, 6.9
- B) 6.4 only
- C) 6.5 Initiating, 6.6 Controlling and 6.8 Closing or terminating a project
- D) 6.7 Managing delivery

**Answer: C**

**Q63.** Which integrated practice is associated with the work package leader?

- A) 6.6 Controlling a project
- B) 6.7 Managing delivery
- C) 6.5 Initiating a project
- D) 6.9 Post-project activities

**Answer: B**

**Q64.** The _project board_ (4.5.3):

- A) Is mandatory in all projects
- B) If needed, should contribute by providing direction and guidance to the project sponsor
- C) Reports to the project manager
- D) Is responsible for delivering work packages

**Answer: B** — Note "**if needed**" — a project board is not always required.

**Q65.** A project board can be either:

**Q66.** The project board should:

**Q67.** Commonly used alternative terms for a project board include:

- A) Project executive, product owner, senior responsible owner
- B) Project steering group, project steering board, project steering committee, governance committee
- C) Project office, PMO, competence centre

**Answer: B** — (Option A lists alternative terms for the _project sponsor_.)

**Q68.** The _project sponsor_ (4.5.4) is accountable to:

# APPENDIX A — Slides marked "EXTRA MATERIAL"

> The lecturer states this material **will not be in the quiz**. Included only for completeness and deeper understanding.

**A1.** _Cohesiveness_ is defined as the degree to which members are attracted to each other and are motivated to stay in the group. Cohesiveness relates to performance **only with adequate work ethics (performance norms)** — high cohesiveness + high performance norms = high productivity; high cohesiveness + low performance norms = low productivity.

**A2.** How to create cohesive teams: make smaller teams; encourage agreement; increase time spent together; increase team status and perceived difficulty to achieve goals; give rewards to teams not individuals; physically isolate the teams.

**A3.** _Diversity_ — advantages: different perspectives create innovative ideas; surface-level diversity may encourage search for deep-level diversity; hard to create homogeneous groups; reduces groupthinking. Disadvantages: conflicts, miscommunication, time consuming. Diversity is advantageous if the task demands diversity and the team can overcome conflicts.

**A4.** _Size_ — groups needing diversity of input/fact-finding can be large (>12) if interdependence is minimised; teams that create solutions and make things happen should be smaller, **5 to 7 members**; if many people are needed, create sub-teams.

**A5.** _Social loafing_ = free riders. Avoid by: developing a common purpose (buy-in); increasing intergroup competition; peer evaluation; giving people a fair share of work; providing means to identify individual efforts and distributing rewards accordingly.

**A6.** _Roles_ = "set of expected behaviour patterns attributed to someone occupying a given position in a social unit" (Robbins and Judge, 2010). **Role conflict** = compliance with one role's requirements makes it difficult to comply with another. **Role identity** = behaviour consistent with the role. **Role perceptions** = our own view of how we should act; **role expectations** = others' view of how we should act; alignment = the _psychological contract_. Zimbardo's experiment illustrates role incorporation.

**A7.** _Expectancy theory_: **MF = V × I × E**. Expectancy (E→P) = probability effort leads to performance; Instrumentality (P→O) = probability performance leads to outcome; Valence (V) = outcome has positive or negative value to employee. Assumes behaviour is entirely rational.

**A8.** _McClelland's three acquired needs_: need for achievement, need for affiliation, need for power.

**A9.** _Reinforcement (Skinner)_ take-aways: be specific; be immediate; make targets achievable; remember the intangible (praise may motivate more than pay); make it unpredictable. Adding rewards is generally more effective than removing punishments; aversive feedback is less effective without a route to improve; too much reinforcement can reduce intrinsic motivation.

**A10.** _Mary Parker Follett_ (1868–1933) — "Mother of management"; conflict is inevitable in groups but must be handled so a group achieves integrity/unity; **power _with_ instead of power _over_**; one person should not give orders — both should agree to take their orders from the situation.

**A11.** _Self-awareness grid_ (I'm OK / I'm not OK × You are OK / You are not OK): Assertive (I care about myself and you); Aggressive (I care about myself, not you); Passive (I don't care about myself, I care about you); Passive-Aggressive (I don't care about either).

**A12.** _Shannon/Weaver (1949) model_: Encoder–Interpreter–Decoder at each end, a medium in between, and noise interfering.

**A13.** _Individual motivation — other factors_: location; length of service; new teams; previous environment.

**A14.** _David Allen — Getting Things Done (GTD)_: "do it, delegate it, defer it, drop it"; the two-minute rule.

**A15.** _Stephen Covey — 7 Habits_: Private Victory (1. Be Proactive, 2. Begin with the end in mind, 3. Put First Things First); Public Victory (4. Think Win/Win, 5. Seek first to understand then to be understood, 6. Synergize); 7. Sharpen the Saw.

**A16.** _Time management_: proactive (looking beyond what has to be done now, problem prevention); reactive (fire-fighting, rewarding but stressful); inactive (resting time is important, should not include thinking time). Goals should be **SMART** — specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-framed — plus accountable, regularly reviewed, stretching, exciting.

- _Reporting_ (7.15) = status, variances, forecasts · _Communication_ (7.13) = enabling effective stakeholder interactions
- Slides list **three** stakeholder directions (downward/upward/outward); the handbook adds **inward** (ourselves)
